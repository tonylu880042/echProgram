What is happening in treadmills right now
The market is moving away from old labels like “Program 1,” “Hill,” and “Interval.” Four things stand out:

Incline walking is huge. NordicTrack is actively positioning incline walking around calorie demand and glute/hamstring engagement, with its incline products going as high as 40%. (NordicTrack)

Personalized intensity is becoming table stakes. iFIT's current SmartAdjust changes treadmill speed and incline automatically and learns from workout history. ActivePulse can further modify intensity based on heart rate. (NordicTrack)

Peloton is personalizing pace. Its newer Pace Targets translate instructor cues into personalized speed ranges for running, walking, Walk+Run, hiking and bootcamp. Peloton also continues to emphasize Scenic and game-style Lanebreak experiences. (Peloton)

Hybrid/functional training is hot. HYROX-style training continues to grow, combining repeated running with strength movements. (Verywell Health)

So I would not launch Echelon with 10 generic treadmill programs. I'd make the program names immediately communicate what the customer wants from the workout.

FAT BURN should be smarter than a generic “fat-burning zone”
I would be careful about promising that a particular workout uniquely “burns fat.” Instead, the consumer-facing promise can be sustained calorie-burning work without requiring hard running.

FAT BURN — 30 MIN

The treadmill alternates between moderate incline walking and light running:

WARM UP → CLIMB → PUSH → RECOVER → CLIMB → PUSH → BIG CLIMB → FINISH

For example:

0–5: 3.0 mph / 1%
5–8: 3.3 / 5%

11–14: 4.5 / 3%
14–17: 3.2 / 10%
17–20: 5.0 / 2%
20–23: 3.3 / 12%
23–26: 5.2 / 2%
26–30: cooldown

The important thing is that Echelon Adapt scales those numbers to the individual.
